# How Nudge differs from Salesforce's AI features

Last reviewed: 15 Nov 2026. The Salesforce column is based on public pricing pages and third-party write-ups (linked at the bottom). Salesforce changes its packaging often, and some sources disagree on which edition includes what, so check the current Salesforce pricing page before quoting any of this to a customer.

Nudge is not a CRM and does not try to be one. It does one job: **upload → insights → export**.

| Area | Salesforce AI (Sales Cloud Einstein, Agentforce) | Nudge |
|---|---|---|
| What you need first | A Salesforce CRM with your accounts, opportunities and activities kept up to date in it. Sources say the advanced AI features also depend on Data Cloud. | An Excel sales file. Word and PowerPoint plans are optional. |
| Time to first insight | An implementation project: licences, data model, admin setup, user training. | Minutes. Upload a file and the brief appears. |
| Who can run it | Needs a Salesforce administrator, and often a partner. | A salesperson with a phone. No administrator. |
| How answers are produced | Machine-learning scores and generative AI (large language models). | Fixed rules and arithmetic. No AI model at all. |
| Can you check an answer? | Scores come with contributing factors, but the model itself is not something a salesperson can recalculate. | Every reply has "How I got this": the numbers used, the rule and the source file. Anyone can redo the sum. |
| Where your data goes | Stored and processed in Salesforce's cloud. | Stays on the phone. Nothing is uploaded; the app works with no signal once installed. |
| Next best action | Recommendations built by an admin as strategies and flows, shown inside CRM record pages. | A daily plan of 3 visits in order, with the reason and the words to say, generated automatically. |
| Scoring | Lead and opportunity scoring, which needs opportunity records and enough history to train on. | Customer-level scores (chance to close, risk of leaving) from invoices alone. No pipeline data needed. |
| Campaigns | Managed as campaign records and members; linking an offer to a saving for one customer takes configuration. | Reads the campaign terms and tells the salesperson which customers have not used an offer, what they would save, and when it ends. |
| Visit notes | Typed into the CRM; call and email capture and conversation analysis are separate features. | Tap keywords, speak or type. The note changes the scores and the pitch immediately. |
| Output for meetings | Dashboards and reports inside Salesforce; exporting a polished one-pager usually means building it. | One tap: customer one-pager (internal and customer-facing) or a meeting pack for the day's visits in PDF, Word or PowerPoint; morning brief; portfolio in Excel; review deck in PowerPoint. Share straight from the phone. |
| Cost | Per user per month. Salesforce's own pricing pages list its AI-inclusive sales editions at several hundred US dollars per user per month, with usage-based Data Cloud credits on top according to third-party sources. | No licence in the pilot. A static website with no server to run. |
| Interface | A full CRM with many objects, tabs and settings. | One chat screen, one revenue screen, one export menu. |

## What Nudge deliberately does not do

Being simple has a price. Nudge has no shared database, no login, no pipeline or opportunity tracking, no email or calendar integration, no forecasting, and no live sync between phones. If you need those, you need a CRM. Nudge can sit beside one: export the sales data from the CRM, upload it to Nudge, and take the brief and one-pagers back out.

## The one-line pitch

Salesforce AI is powerful if you already live in Salesforce and have people to run it. Nudge is for teams who have a sales spreadsheet and want to know, this morning, who to see and what to say, and be able to check why.
